    Inputs & Financial Terminology Guide

    How the Inputs Work

    Exchange Toggle (NSE .NS / BSE .BO)
    Switches the search dropdown between India's National Stock Exchange (.NS) and Bombay Stock Exchange (.BO).
    Select Indian Stocks (2–10)
    Search by company name or ticker symbol and click to add or remove stocks. You need at least 2 stocks to measure correlation.
    Custom Weights (optional)
    Comma-separated numbers matching your stock count (e.g., 30, 25, 25, 20). Automatically normalized to 100%; leave blank for equal weights.
    Portfolio Value (INR)
    Total capital invested in ₹ (minimum ₹1,000). Converts percentage risk into rupee loss amounts.
    Lookback Period (2y / 3y / 5y)
    Historical daily price window fetched from Yahoo Finance to estimate returns, volatility, and correlations.
    Holding Days
    Risk horizon in trading days (default 21 ≈ 1 month; range 1–252). Daily simulated returns are compounded over this period.
    Confidence Level (90% / 95% / 99%)
    Defines the tail cutoff. At 95%, VaR and ES measure the worst 5% of simulated outcomes.
    Monte Carlo Paths
    Number of simulated future scenarios generated (default 10,000; range 500–50,000).
    Use Cholesky Correlation Matrix
    When checked, simulated shocks move together based on historical correlations. Uncheck to see how much risk is hidden if you assume stocks are independent.
    Stress Blend (α)
    Slides correlations from historical (0.00) toward a market-crash regime (0.97) via R(α) = (1 − α)Rhist + αJ.

    Key Terminologies

    Monte Carlo Simulation
    Generates thousands of random future return paths using historical mean, volatility, and correlation to map the full distribution of possible portfolio outcomes.
    Cholesky Decomposition
    A matrix factorization (R = L · LT) that transforms independent random normal shocks into correlated shocks matching your stocks' real-world co-movement.
    Annualized Return & Volatility
    Scales daily average return by 252 trading days and daily standard deviation by √252 to express expected return and risk on a yearly basis.
    Correlation (ρ) & Diversification
    Measures how two stocks move together (-1 to +1). Lower correlation reduces portfolio swings because losses in one stock can be offset by another.
    Correlation Breakdown
    During market panics, correlations spike toward +1 simultaneously, causing diversification to fail when it is needed most.
    Value at Risk (VaR)
    The loss threshold at your chosen confidence level (e.g., 5th percentile at 95% confidence).
    Expected Shortfall (ES / CVaR)
    The average loss across all tail outcomes worse than VaR, capturing extreme crash severity.
